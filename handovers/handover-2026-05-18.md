## Key Findings / Lessons Learned

### 1. PTO over-twist during furl — the primary furl failure mode
During furl the hub decelerates (less wind power at higher elevation angle) while the PTO ground ring free-spins on inertia. MPPT torque `τ_gen = k_mppt × ω_hub²` → 0 as ω_hub → 0, so there is effectively zero generator braking. Result: Δα accumulated to 182.9° (observed in dashboard), well into the torsional-collapse regime where line slack cascades and the structure loses integrity.

**Fix applied:** Angular damping on all ring omegas ramped with `release_frac` during the furl loop. Represents the generator being switched to plugging/regenerative brake as the backline pays out.

**Note:** The brief hub reversal (ω_hub = −0.14 rad/s) observed in the screenshots is a **Tulloch torsional wave** — elastic energy released as the TRPT geometry changes during the rise — NOT a sign of lines crossing and driving in reverse. Do not repeat the incorrect "lines cross → reverse torque" explanation.

### 2. Ring buckling display is wrong during furl — serious limitation
`ring_safety_frame()` in `structural_safety.jl` assumes:
- All n lines are uniformly taut (symmetric loading)
- The polygon compression formula `N_comp = F_v / (2·tan(π/n))` where `F_v = F_inward / n`

**Why this is wrong during furl:**
- Ring beam compression arises from the **inward radial component** of line tension, which is proportional to **sin(Δα_segment)** — the per-segment twist angle. When torque drops (furl, deceleration) the lines become more axial; even taut lines exert near-zero inward radial force.
- Slack lines contribute zero (correct by construction), but the formula treats the total F_inward from any remaining taut lines as if distributed symmetrically across all n vertices.
- If only 2 of 5 lines are taut (one side), the worst-case beam segment adjacent to those vertices carries up to n/2 = 2.5× more compression than `N_comp` reports. The formula UNDER-reports the real peak and shows RED misleadingly on the whole ring.

**Current state:** Limitation commented in `structural_safety.jl` with explanation. HUD label updated to "⚠ steady-state only". Tests not yet run; commit pending.

**Future fix options (in order of complexity):**
- A. Grey out / suppress ring colour when more than ~30% of attached lines are slack (simple threshold)
- B. Per-vertex force accumulation: compute F_v for each of the n vertices separately, take the maximum, use that for N_comp (correct for asymmetric loading)
- C. Full 2-D polygon frame solve per ring (most accurate, most complex)

### 3. Bearing transverse damping — value needs observation
`bearing_tr_damp = 0.99994` per step at dt=4e-5 → τ ≈ 0.67 s. User acknowledged this seems like "a lot" and wanted to observe before tuning. The physics: air resistance on fitting hardware, Dyneema viscoelastic damping, gyroscopic stabilisation from the spinning rotor. **Not** a sliding rail or shaft tube — the bearing is a free particle in the air.

Time constant reference table:
```
0.99994 /step  →  τ ≈ 0.67 s   (current)
0.99997 /step  →  τ ≈ 1.3 s
0.99999 /step  →  τ ≈ 4 s
0.999998 /step →  τ ≈ 20 s
```
