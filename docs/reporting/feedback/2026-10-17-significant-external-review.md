\## Summary

This internal engineering report presents a 930-candidate simulation design screen for a 5 kW-class, ground-generation rotary kite turbine utilizing a Tensile Rotary Power Transmission (TRPT) shaft. By coupling a three-island Differential Evolution (DE) optimizer with a multibody Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) solver and Blade Element Momentum (BEM) aerodynamics (incorporating a calibrated \$\\cos\^{2.65}\$ bank derate), the study explores continuous and discrete geometric parameters---including ring radii, spacing, polygon line count, rotor count, blade bank angle, and blade scale---under simultaneous power-floor, tubular ring beam-column buckling, blade-tip clearance, and geometric over-twist gates. The campaign identifies a feasible 6-ring, 3-line single-rotor winning geometry that delivers a mean power of 5.309 kW at an airborne mass of 25.698 kg (27.364 kg-equivalent penalized objective score), while maintaining a 15.0 minimum structural factor of safety on the topmost ring and a 2.03 minimum segment torque margin, accompanied by commit-level data provenance and a transparent engineering failure ledger.

\## Strengths

\* Coupling a multi-island Differential Evolution optimizer directly with time-domain coupled multibody ODE simulations to simultaneously screen continuous geometric parameters and discrete topologies (polygon count and active rotor count) under closed-form tubular beam-column buckling, geometric over-twist, and blade-tip clearance gates represents a highly novel and practical methodological contribution for rotary airborne wind energy design.

\* The calibration of an empirical \$\\cos\^{2.65}\$ cosine power-law bank derate against 3D pre-cone BEM sweeps provides a physically well-motivated, computationally efficient reduced-order surrogate for annular ring-mounted rotors that prevents the evolutionary optimizer from exploiting unmodeled coning aerodynamics.

\* The report exhibits exceptional scientific transparency and auditability through its commit-pinned numbers register, explicit documentation of model boundaries, and a detailed failure ledger that isolates simulation-in-the-loop artifacts such as static-to-dynamic handoff rope-break false rejections.

\## Weaknesses

\* The reported minimum Factor of Safety (15.0 on Ring 6), airborne mass (25.698 kg), and potential mass-reduction headroom are only conditionally valid within the bounded screen because the simulation sets the blade-root structural Factor of Safety to infinity, defers the beam-on-supports blade integrity model, and omits outer tether-to-blade stiffening bridles whose structural mass or parasitic aerodynamic drag at the rotor perimeter could erode the narrow +6.28% power margin and S1 torsional margin; furthermore, full structural qualification for a 5 kW turbine of this swept area would benefit from evaluation against standard transient gust, direction-change, and fault design load cases (Reference Paper: IEC 61400-2: Wind Turbines -- Part 2: Design Requirements for Small Wind Turbines, International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC), 2013).

\* While the winning candidate satisfies power and rotational stationarity over the 40-second measurement window, the vertical shaft sag (bowing under gravity) grows continuously from 0.209 m to 0.613 m at the hub during the window and reaches 0.833 m in a post-window extension run, indicating that transverse gravitational degrees of freedom have not reached a strictly stationary spatial equilibrium and motivating comparison against continuum Cosserat-rod or finite-element tensegrity equilibrium formulations (Reference Paper: Dynamic analysis of the tensegrity structure of a rotary airborne wind energy machine / Dynamic Stability of a Rotary Airborne Wind Energy System, Gonzalo Sánchez-Arriaga, Álvaro Cerrillo-Vacas, David Unterweger, and Christof Beaupoil, 2024).

\* The coupled system equilibrium relies on an idealized boundary condition that prescribes a constant 324.1 N lift-line tension at a fixed 70-degree elevation---scaled solely from 1.5 times the static airborne weight---rather than dynamically modeling the coupled aerodynamic rotor thrust, tether drag, overturning moment equilibrium, and active autogyro stack modulation required across varying wind speeds.

\* Several methodological details, telemetry diagnostics, and draft release items remain incomplete in the current text, including explicit decoding equations and search bounds for the 10-gene vector, the analytical power-sizing formula for the reference rotor radius (\`r_rotor\`), the unlogged failure causes for 201 of the 301 plain \`reject\` designs, and an internal discrepancy between the quoted window-minimum Factor of Safety (\`15.0\`) and its signed register value (\`14.972\` in \`NR-009\`).
