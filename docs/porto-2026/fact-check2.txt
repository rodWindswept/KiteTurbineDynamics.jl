# KTDPaper_draft.md — Fact-Check #2: Abstract References
## 2026-06-23

The abstract claims:
  "This gap is an order of magnitude larger than the 18–21% overprediction
   consensus in conventional AWE literature (Kheiri et al. 2018, Carceller
   Candau 2022, Pfister & Blondel 2020)."

### Rachel Leuthold — SHOULD BE ADDED

Her 2019 MAWES induction model (with Crawford, Gros & Diehl) directly quantifies
how wake/induction effects cause power prediction errors in multi-kite systems:
  Leuthold, R., Crawford, C., Gros, S., & Diehl, M. (2019).
  "Engineering wake induction model for axisymmetric multi-kite airborne wind
   energy systems." Wake Conference 2019.

This is the engineering-model counterpart to the empirical percentages from
Kheiri, Carceller, and Pfister. The abstract currently cites only the empirical
papers but misses the theoretical/modelling paper that explains *why* the gap
exists structurally. Adding Leuthold would strengthen the "consensus" framing —
it's not just three scattered empirical observations, it's a known structural
problem with analytical induction simplifications.

The paper already discusses induction models and wake effects in the Introduction
(§1, lines 21-22): "Kheiri et al. (2018) identified induction factor effects..."
Leuthold's work is the natural companion to that sentence.

Suggested fix:
  "...larger than the 18–21% overprediction consensus in conventional AWE
   literature (Kheiri et al. 2018, Leuthold et al. 2019, Carceller Candau
   2022, Pfister & Blondel 2020)."

### Elena Malz — PROBABLY NOT FOR THE ABSTRACT

Her 2018 6-DOF reference model (0.92% energy error vs AP2 flight data) and
her PhD power optimization work are about validation accuracy and model fidelity,
not systematic overprediction. Different claim.

Relevant papers:
  Malz, E.C., Verendel, V., & Gros, S. (2018). "Computing the power profiles
   for an airborne wind energy system based on large-scale wind data."
  Malz, E. (2020). "Airborne wind energy — to fly or not to fly? A study on
   power production and electricity system integration." PhD thesis, Chalmers.

These would fit better in the Introduction or Discussion where model fidelity
is discussed, but not in the abstract's tight "18-21% consensus" sentence.
