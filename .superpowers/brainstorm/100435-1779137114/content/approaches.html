Three Strategic Approaches

How to position KiteTurbineDynamics.jl for partnership engagement, grants, and publication

A

Open Research Platform

Frame: KiteTurbineDynamics.jl becomes the shared simulation infrastructure for an AWE consortium. Publish the emergent torsion physics open-access. Invite SomeAWE + Freiburg to co-develop the rotary lifter module as a joint tool.

Grant ask: Shared tooling + CFD validation + small demonstrator. Consortium owns the platform together.

Publications: Emergent torsion paper (novel physics) + optimisation campaign paper (engineering methods).

Pros

  • Broadest coalition — every partner has a reason to join
  • Open-source posture builds academic credibility fast
  • Natural fit for SomeAWE + Freiburg who share the rotary lifter problem
  • Matches "shared agenda" framing partners want to see

Cons

  • IP is more distributed — harder to protect core innovations
  • Requires active community stewardship
B

Technology Maturation Programme

Frame: The simulator proves TRL 3–4 readiness. The grant buys TRL 5–6: CFD validation, structural testing, small demonstrator. Lead with the 50 kW commercial case as the destination. NZTC / Shell Gamechanger as industry anchor.

Grant ask: Staged maturation: simulation → validation → prototype. Clear milestone-gated deliverables.

Publications: AWE Forum conference paper (near-term) + journal paper after CFD validation.

Pros

  • Clearest commercial story — partners see the 50 kW destination
  • NZTC / Shell relationships are ready to activate
  • Milestone structure is what grant bodies like Innovate UK want

Cons

  • Needs an industry anchor to lead — you may not control timeline
  • Academic partners (Freiburg) less naturally incentivised
  • Higher IP protection burden before sharing data
C

Rotary Lifter Consortium

Frame: SomeAWE, Freiburg, and Windswept are all working on rotary lift devices. KiteTurbineDynamics.jl provides the system-level context — the full kite turbine ODE — that the others lack. Build a targeted 3-party consortium around this one shared technical problem.

Grant ask: Joint rotary lifter programme: shared modelling, hardware tests, shared IP. Narrower scope than A or B but highest probability of forming quickly.

Publications: Rotary lifter comparison paper (3 architectures, simulation vs experiment).

Pros

  • Tightest natural coalition — shared problem already exists
  • Fastest to form — existing relationships with SomeAWE
  • Simulation already demonstrates 8× lift stability advantage
  • Drone + marine partners also fit here (tethered rotor expertise)

Cons

  • Narrower scope — may not unlock larger grant funding
  • Rotary lifter is a means, not the full commercial story

Recommendation

Start with C to form the consortium, layer in A as the platform framing, use B as the commercial story for grant bodies. The rotary lifter is the natural hook to get SomeAWE and Freiburg into the room. Once they're in, the open platform framing keeps them engaged. The commercial maturation story is what unlocks Innovate UK / NZTC money.
